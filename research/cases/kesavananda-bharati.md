# Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala

## Case Identification

**Case:** His Holiness Kesavananda Bharati Sripadagalvaru & Ors. v. State of Kerala & Anr.

**Court:** Supreme Court of India

**Date:** 24 April 1973

**Case Number:** W.P.(C) No. 135 of 1970

**Citation:** (1973) 4 SCC 225; [1973] Supp. SCR 1

**Bench:** 13-Judge Constitution Bench

## Background

The petitions challenged constitutional amendments including the Twenty-fourth, Twenty-fifth and Twenty-ninth Amendments.

The principal constitutional question concerned the extent of Parliament's power to amend the Constitution under Article 368.

## Legal Issues

1. What is the scope of Parliament's constituent power under Article 368?
2. Can Parliament amend Fundamental Rights?
3. Are there substantive limitations on the constitutional amendment power?

## Relevant Constitutional Provisions

- Article 13
- Article 25
- Article 26
- Article 31
- Article 32
- Article 368

## Decision

The Supreme Court held, by a narrow majority, that Parliament's power to amend the Constitution is wide but is subject to limitations derived from the Constitution's basic structure.

The decision is associated with the **basic structure doctrine**.

## Ratio Decidendi

The constitutional amendment power cannot be used to destroy or alter the Constitution's basic structure.

The exact identification of basic features depends upon the constitutional principle under consideration and subsequent judicial development.

## Important Constitutional Principles
