# Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India

## Case Identification

**Case:** Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India

**Court:** Supreme Court of India

**Date:** 25 January 1978

**Citation:** (1978) 1 SCC 248

## Background

The case concerned the impounding of Maneka Gandhi's passport under the Passports Act, 1967.

The petitioner challenged the action and raised important questions concerning personal liberty and the relationship between Articles 14, 19 and 21 of the Constitution.

## Legal Issues

1. Whether the procedure depriving a person of personal liberty must satisfy constitutional requirements of fairness.
2. Whether Articles 14, 19 and 21 operate independently or have an interrelationship.
3. Whether administrative action affecting personal liberty is subject to constitutional scrutiny.

## Relevant Constitutional Provisions

- Article 14
- Article 19
- Article 21
- Article 32

## Decision

The Supreme Court interpreted Article 21 broadly and held that the procedure established by law must satisfy constitutional requirements of fairness and reasonableness.

The judgment significantly developed the relationship between Articles 14, 19 and 21.

## Ratio Decidendi

A law prescribing a procedure that deprives a person of personal liberty must meet constitutional requirements.

The judgment rejected an interpretation of Article 21 that would permit any formally enacted procedure to deprive personal liberty without substantive constitutional scrutiny.
